10 Science-Backed Ways to Sharpen Your Thinking

You cannot change your core intelligence overnight, but you can improve how well your brain performs day to day. These habits are supported by research on cognition, and they also help you do better on reasoning tests.

  1. Sleep 7 to 9 hours. Sleep helps the brain consolidate memories and reset attention. Even one short night lowers focus and problem-solving.
  2. Exercise regularly. Aerobic activity such as brisk walking improves blood flow to the brain and is linked to better memory and executive function.
  3. Learn something new. A language, an instrument or a new skill challenges your brain in new ways.
  4. Practice puzzle types. Number sequences, logic grids and analogies become faster once the patterns feel familiar.
  5. Read widely. Reading builds vocabulary and background knowledge, which support verbal reasoning.
  6. Eat well. A balanced diet with vegetables, fruit, whole grains, fish and nuts supports long-term brain health.
  7. Stay socially active. Conversation and debate exercise memory, attention and perspective-taking.
  8. Manage stress. Chronic stress hurts memory and concentration. Breaks, walks, breathing exercises and hobbies help.
  9. Teach what you learn. Explaining an idea to someone else is one of the fastest ways to understand it deeply.
  10. Review your mistakes. After any test or puzzle, work out why the right answer is right. That is where most learning happens.

A note on "brain training" apps

Research shows that practicing a task makes you better at that task, but the benefits do not always transfer to general intelligence. Use puzzles because they are enjoyable and useful practice, and combine them with the lifestyle habits above for the best overall effect.
